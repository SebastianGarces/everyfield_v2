1 · The ruling that already exists

product-docs/decisions.md, ledger row 187 (the sharing half), verified today:

  • A plant that joins via a sending-church or network invitation defaults ALL sharing toggles ON — the six pull toggles and the push toggle alike.
  • Consent moves to the acceptance moment: the screen states in plain language what the overseer will see, before the planter accepts. DB defaults stay FALSE; the ON write is application-level in acceptInvitation.
  • A self-started plant stays all-OFF.
  • The planter can turn any toggle off at any time. Turning one off notifies the overseer's admins with coarse wording ("changed what it shares with you"), never per-toggle — per-toggle wording maximizes social pressure and poisons the relationship.

That last line answers the question the 08-18 session left open ("maybe planters cannot turn those off"): the ledger says they can, with coarse notify. And it is very close to what Bryan asked for — the sharing agreement established up front, at acceptance, rather than only as individual toggles.

"The planter controls what is shared, and it is off by default." — rubric v0 §7, stated universally

Maybe the sharing agreement is established up front by the planter and sending organization rather than handled only as individual toggles. — Bryan, C10

2 · Rubric v1 §7 delta (into the D0 draft, assuming D1 = ledger)

  • §7's universal "off by default" splits into the two real cases: self-started plants share nothing until the planter turns it on; plants that accepted a sending-org invitation start with sharing on, agreed at the acceptance screen.
  • Both keep planter control: any toggle can be turned off at any time; the rubric notes the org is notified in coarse terms — so the judge never treats sharing state as secret or surprising.
  • When a category is off, its observations disappear from the network view — unchanged — and the unshared posture reads "Limited visibility" (cross-reference to #480, which owns that rule).
  • The judge may not describe sharing as "off by default" as a universal fact, and may not editorialize about a plant's sharing choices to either audience.
